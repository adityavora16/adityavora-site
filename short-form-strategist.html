What a short-form strategist does

A short-form strategist decides what a channel should make, then checks every week whether it worked. Most of my week goes on retention graphs. Each one shows the second viewers gave up, and once you put thirty of them side by side the same moments keep turning up: a slow first line, a setup that runs long, a payoff that arrives after people have already gone.

That reading decides which formats and hooks stay on the channel and which get dropped. A format that pulls a big number once but loses people halfway through rarely deserves another go. A quieter one that people watch to the end usually deserves several.

Then I write the brief the team works from. It covers the hook, the order of the beats, where each cut falls and what goes on screen, so writers and editors aren't left guessing. Once the video is out, I track its weekly numbers against that brief, so the next one starts from what the last one taught us.

A short-form strategist is a different job from a social media manager, who schedules posts and answers comments. It's also different from a video editor, though I work with editors every day and give them direction. And I don't run paid distribution. If ads are what you need, hire someone who buys media.

Who it is for, and who it is not

The work suits creators who post on a steady schedule and still see results swing from one video to the next. Founders building a personal channel get a lot out of it too, because the channel has to sound like them and still hold strangers. So do brands whose in-house team is capable but has never had a written spec to work to.

I'm the wrong person if you want volume without a point of view, because I'll keep asking you to make fewer videos. I'm also the wrong person if your real problem is paid distribution. If you post a couple of times a month, a retainer is more than you need; buy the audit instead.

What each one gets you

Content audit

I go through every video you've published and score each one on its opening, the points where viewers drop off, and its format. It all goes into a spreadsheet with every post tagged, along with a list of fixes in the order I'd make them. Then we spend 45 minutes on a call working through it.

Strategy call

Forty-five minutes on how you're positioned, which formats suit you and how your hooks should open. Within a day you'll have a written plan to work from.

Retention teardown

You send three videos and their retention graphs. I go through them before we meet, then show you the exact points where viewers leave and how I'd cut the same footage to keep them.

Team workshop

Two hours with your in-house team on writing hooks, reading a retention graph and setting up review loops, so the standard holds once I'm off the call. I've taught versions of it at LIT School, Terribly Tiny Tales, IPLIX Media and WebVeda.

Monthly retainer

I take on the scripting, direct your editors, handle the posting and track the numbers every week. This is the one where I work as part of your team.
