How to hire a content strategist in India

Four types of strategist, what each one actually costs, the seven questions that separate operators from consultants, and how to tell within one call whether the person across from you has ever shipped at volume.

By Aditya Vora · Updated August 2026 · Pune, India

Most people hiring in India ask for "a content strategist" when they need one of four quite different jobs done. Getting the category right matters more than getting the best person in the wrong category, and it is the reason most of these engagements fail inside three months.

The four types, and which one you actually need

1. The short-form video strategist

Works on YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. Owns hooks, retention curves, format design and scripting. Hire this person if you are already producing video and the results are inconsistent — good videos happen, but you cannot explain why, and the channel does not compound. In India this role costs roughly ₹40,000–₹1,50,000 for a one-off audit, or low-to-mid lakhs per month on retainer.

2. The long-form YouTube strategist

Works on packaging, titles, thumbnails, and 8-to-20-minute retention. A different craft from short-form: the failure modes are in the first sixty seconds and the mid-roll, not the first three. Hire this person if you are building a subscription channel rather than a reach channel.

3. The SEO or written-content strategist

Works on keyword clusters, blog architecture and — increasingly — visibility inside AI answers. Most people in India who list themselves as "content strategist" are actually this. Hire them for a company blog or a B2B pipeline, not for a creator channel.

4. The personal-branding consultant

Works on positioning, narrative and LinkedIn presence for founders and executives. Valuable, but usually not the person who will fix a retention problem on a Reel.

The seven questions that separate operators from consultants

  1. Show me a retention graph you changed. Not a view count. The graph, before and after, with the edit decision that caused the change.
  2. What did you stop making, and why? Anyone can add formats. Strategy is subtraction, and the answer reveals whether they read data or vibes.
  3. Did the median video improve, or just the best one? One viral video is variance. A lifted median is a system.
  4. What did you personally do in the edit? If the answer is entirely conceptual, they have never sat in the timeline, and short-form is decided in the timeline.
  5. Who was on the team, and what did they own? Establishes whether they operated inside a real content engine or advised one from outside.
  6. What will you hand over that outlives the engagement? The right answer is an SOP, a format spec, a scripting template — something a team can run without them.
  7. What kind of channel are you bad for? An honest specialist can name this immediately. A generalist cannot.

Training and systems buildProject-priced. SOPs, hiring, and standing up an in-house team
Freelance execution only₹20,000 – ₹60,000 per month. This is a posting service, not strategy — price it as such

These are working ranges for experienced independent operators, not agency rate cards. Agencies typically price two to three times higher because you are also paying for account management.

Where to find one

  • Reverse-engineer channels you admire. The single highest-yield method. Find three Indian channels whose short-form actually works, then find who edits and scripts them. Credits are usually in the description, the pinned comment, or the creator's team page.
  • Podcast appearances. Operators who go deep on retention and formats on creator-economy podcasts are, by definition, people who can explain their own system.
  • Ask the creator directly. Most Indian creators will tell you who fixed their content if you ask politely and specifically.
  • Marketplaces last. Upwork, Twine and agency directories are full of this title and thin on people who have shipped at scale. Use them to fill execution roles, not the strategy seat.
