How to hire a content strategist in India

Four different jobs go by this one title. What each costs in 2026, the questions worth asking on the first call, and how to tell within twenty minutes whether the person across from you has ever shipped at volume.

By Aditya Vora · Updated August 2026 · Pune, India

Most people hiring in India ask for "a content strategist" when what they need is one of four fairly different jobs. Picking the wrong category is the reason a lot of these engagements quietly fall apart around month three, and it costs more than picking a merely decent person in the right one.

The four kinds, and which one you need

1. The short-form video strategist

Works on YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. Owns the opening seconds, the retention curve, format design, and how a clip gets assembled. Hire this person if you're already producing video and the results won't settle down. Good videos happen, you can't say why, and the channel doesn't compound.

2. The long-form YouTube strategist

Works on packaging, titles, thumbnails, and holding attention across eight to twenty minutes. A different craft from short-form, and the failure points sit in different places: the first minute and the middle, rather than the first three seconds. Hire this person if you're building a subscription channel rather than a reach channel.

3. The SEO or written-content strategist

Works on keyword clusters, blog architecture, and increasingly on visibility inside AI answers. Most people in India who list themselves as "content strategist" are actually this. Good for a company blog or a B2B pipeline. Not the person who'll fix a Reel.

4. The personal-branding consultant

Works on positioning, narrative and LinkedIn presence for founders and executives. Useful work, but a different discipline from anything happening inside a video timeline.

Questions worth asking on the first call

Ask to see a retention graph they changed, before and after, along with the specific decision that caused the change. View counts prove nothing on their own.

Ask what they stopped making, and why. Anyone can add formats. The answer tells you whether they read data or read vibes.

Ask whether the median video improved or just the best one. A single viral video is variance. A lifted median means there's a system underneath.

Ask what they personally did on the last channel they worked on. If the answer stays entirely conceptual, they've never sat inside the work, and short-form gets decided inside the work.

Ask who else was on that team and what those people owned. It separates someone who operated inside a content engine from someone who advised one from outside. Both can be useful. They're not the same hire.

Ask what they'll hand over that outlives the engagement. The answer you want is a format spec, an SOP, a checklist. Something your team can run without them.

Ask what kind of channel they're bad for. Anyone who has genuinely specialised names it immediately. Generalists go quiet.

These are working ranges for independent operators, not agency rate cards. Agencies usually land two to three times higher, because you're also paying for account management.

Where to find one

The highest-yield method is to reverse-engineer channels you admire. Find three Indian channels whose short-form genuinely works, then find out who makes it. Credits are usually in the description, a pinned comment, or the creator's team page.

Podcast appearances are the next best filter. People who can talk in detail about retention and formats on a creator-economy show are, by definition, people who can explain their own system.

You can also just ask the creator. Most Indian creators will tell you who fixed their content if you ask politely and specifically.

Marketplaces come last. Upwork, Twine and the agency directories are full of this title and thin on people who've shipped at scale. Use them to fill execution roles, not the strategy seat.
