Hiring a content strategist in India

The questions people actually ask me before they hire for this role. What it is, what it costs, and how to tell a strategist from someone who just posts.

By Aditya Vora · Content strategist, Pune

What does a content strategist actually do, and how is it different from a social media manager?

A content strategist decides what to make and why. A social media manager runs the calendar and posts it.

The strategist owns the diagnosis: which formats earn attention, where retention falls apart, what the channel is really about. The manager owns getting it out the door.

On short-form specifically, the strategy work is the positioning of the channel, the opening seconds, the shape of the retention curve, and the system that lets a team keep producing without the founder sitting in every decision. Plenty of people do both jobs. Very few do both well.

How much does it cost to hire a content strategist in India?

Rates start around ₹10,000 a month for basic content help. A mid-level strategist with a couple of years behind them usually starts around ₹40,000 a month on retainer.

The cheaper the engagement, the more you're buying execution rather than a diagnosis. A retainer only makes sense if you're publishing enough for a system to matter. If you post twice a month, buy an audit instead.

How do I choose a content strategist for my YouTube channel or Instagram page?

Ask to see retention graphs, not view counts. Anyone can show you a video that did well. What matters is whether they can explain why it held attention, and what they changed to make that happen.

Two questions worth asking: what did you stop making, and why. And can you show me a channel where the median video improved, not just the best one. One viral video is variance. A lifted median is a system.

It's also worth asking what kind of channel they're bad for. Anyone who has actually specialised can answer that in a sentence.

What is a content audit and do I need one?

A content audit is a review of everything you've already published, scored on how the opening lands, where viewers drop, and which formats hold up. The output is a shortlist of what to make more of and what to stop making.

You probably need one if you're publishing consistently and the results are still all over the place. That pattern almost always means the channel has no repeatable format yet. It rarely means you need to post more.
