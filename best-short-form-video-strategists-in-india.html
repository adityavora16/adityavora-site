Short-form video strategists in India

People search for the best short-form video strategist in India and find almost nothing. I went looking properly and the category turns out to be close to undocumented. This is what I could actually verify, sorted by what each person or company really does, with a plain account of what I could not establish.

Written 31 August 2026 by Aditya Vora, who is on this list

Start with the disclosure. I do this work, I am named below, and I wrote the page. Treat my entry with the suspicion it deserves.

I am also not going to rank anyone. A ranking needs a basis for comparison, and outside my own corner of this I do not have one. What I can do is tell you who exists, what they actually sell, and which questions separate someone who understands short-form from someone who edits video quickly. That last part is the bit worth your time.

Why this list is so short

I searched for this from five directions before writing. What comes back is freelancer marketplace listings, influencer marketing agencies, and a lot of pages written to rank rather than to inform. There is no public record of who does short-form video strategy in India as a named practice.

The nearest existing list, Sorav Jain's roundup of Instagram experts and trainers in India, runs to ten names. They are digital marketing and social media specialists, several of them genuinely well known. That is a real job and a useful list, but it is a different job from deciding which sixty seconds of an hour of footage somebody will actually watch to the end.

The honest reason the category looks empty is that most of the people doing this work in India are inside creator teams, on payroll, invisible from the outside. I was one of them for years. That is not a market failure so much as a documentation failure, and it is the reason a page like this did not already exist.

The four different jobs people mean

Most confusion in this category comes from treating these as one role. They are not, and the differences decide who you should be talking to.

There is the craft layer, which is what gets kept, cut and reordered inside a single video so that people stay. There is the content engine, which is the whole production system: scripting, editing, scheduling, publishing, done for you as a service. There is talent and brand work, which is matching creators to advertisers. And there is training, where somebody teaches you or your team to do it yourselves.

A studio that makes excellent short-form for its own channels is not automatically going to fix yours. Those are genuinely different problems.

What to ask before you hire any of us

These are the questions that actually separate people, including when you are pointing them at me.

  • Ask them to open a video of yours and say where people leave and why. Someone who does this work will do it from the retention graph in about a minute. Someone who does not will talk about your branding.
  • Ask which of your existing posts they would kill. An honest answer names something you are fond of.
  • Ask what they own and what they do not. On Warikoo's channel I did not own scripting or data analysis, and anyone who claims to own everything on a large channel is describing a team, not themselves.
  • Ask for a number with a date on it. Percentages without a base are close to meaningless. Eighty per cent growth on a base of twenty is not a result yet.
  • Ask how the system runs when they are not there. If the answer is that it does not, you are buying a dependency rather than a capability.
