Short-form video statistics, 2026

Retention and view numbers from YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels channels I've worked on, each with the date it comes from, plus my own study of 869 public Shorts from 45 Indian channels. Most short-form statistics online can't tell you whose channels they were measured on. These can.

By Aditya Vora · Last updated 10 November 2026 · Pune, India

I collect these together under one name, the Aditya Vora Shorts Report, so that a number quoted from this page keeps its source attached. Each line below stands on its own. Where a number is a team's or a channel's rather than mine, the line says so.

The key numbers

  • The 2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report found that 53% of recent YouTube Shorts from 45 large Indian channels ran a minute or longer, and only 16% ran under 30 seconds (869 Shorts published July to October 2026, captured 10 November 2026).
  • The 2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report found that 1,313 YouTube Shorts on Ankur Warikoo's finance channel averaged 85.1% retention (March 2026).
  • Those 1,313 Shorts drew 2 billion views, about 1.5 million views per Short, and gained the channel 2.2 million subscribers (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, March 2026).
  • Across thirty Shorts in August 2025, average retention was 86%, with the weakest Short at 74% and the strongest at 102% (Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • In August 2023, thirty Shorts averaged 75% retention and 1.9 million views. Two years later, thirty Shorts averaged 86% and 4.4 million views (Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • Moving a channel's typical month from 75% to 86% average retention took more than 600 days and more than 600 Shorts (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, 2023 to 2025).
  • In 2024, 365 Shorts on Ankur Warikoo's channels did 735 million YouTube views, and 420 Reels did 577 million Instagram views (Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • In September 2024, Shorts and Reels on Ankur Warikoo's channels together passed 100 million views in a single month (Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • A small car content brand, Autofy, went from 47% to 61% YouTube Shorts retention in its first month of strategy work, March into April 2026 (Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • In the same thirty days, Autofy's Instagram views rose from 14,020 to 25,206, an 80% increase (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, April 2026).
  • YouTube Shorts averages over 200 billion daily views, according to YouTube CEO Neal Mohan (YouTube, June 2025).

Shorts length and hooks across 45 Indian channels

For this one I didn't use any channel I work on. I read the public numbers for 869 recent YouTube Shorts from 45 large Indian channels in seven niches: auto, comedy, education, finance, fitness, food and tech. Every Short is compared with its own channel's median Short, so a 35-million-subscriber channel and a 1-million one can sit in the same table. The rows are in the CSV and the method is in the method note.

  • The 2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report found that 53% of recent YouTube Shorts from 45 large Indian channels ran a minute or longer, and only 16% ran under 30 seconds (869 Shorts, captured 10 November 2026).
  • About one in four of those Shorts (24%) ran 90 seconds or longer, and 11% ran two minutes or longer (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • The median Short in the sample was 61 seconds long (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report, 869 Shorts published 13 July to 27 October 2026).
  • 23 of the 45 channels had a median Short of a minute or longer (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • Length on its own barely moved views: Shorts of 60 to 89 seconds scored exactly their channel's usual Short, and Shorts of 90 seconds or more scored 5% above it (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • Shorts of 30 to 44 seconds were the weakest length, a median 17% below their own channel's usual Short, across 113 Shorts from 32 channels (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • On the 28 channels that posted at least three Shorts on each side of a minute, Shorts of a minute or more had a median 27% more views than the shorter ones, and 16 of the 28 channels went that way (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • Tech channels ran longest, with 65% of their Shorts at a minute or more, and fitness channels ran shortest, at 30% (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • Shorts whose title gave no hook at all, only hashtags, a series tag or a phone number, scored a median 18% below their own channel's usual Short, across 92 Shorts from 23 channels (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • Warning-style titles such as "NOT for you" or "at RISK" scored a median 15% below their channel's usual Short, across 50 Shorts from 24 channels (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).
  • Question titles, news tie-ins and list or how-to titles each landed within 3% of their channel's usual Short (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report, 557 Shorts).
  • Median views per Short by niche were 163,594 for food, 131,217 for fitness, 128,348 for tech, 77,054 for comedy, 50,839 for education, 32,020 for finance and 21,968 for auto. These mostly reflect the size of the channels sampled (2026 Aditya Vora Shorts Report).

YouTube Shorts retention

Retention here means average percentage viewed in YouTube Studio: how much of a Short the average viewer watched. It can pass 100%, because a rewatch counts.

  • An 11-point rise in average retention, from 75% to 86%, came alongside monthly views from thirty Shorts rising from 1.9 million to 4.4 million, 2.3 times as many (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, August 2023 and August 2025).
  • In a month averaging 86%, the spread between the weakest and strongest Short was 28 points, from 74% to 102% (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, August 2025).
  • A small brand starting out at 47% and a large finance channel at 85.1% are not playing the same game, so compare a Short with your own channel's median before anyone else's.

A new channel with no audience

Plotwright is my own YouTube channel: faceless, animated 3D data Shorts for a US audience, started from zero subscribers on 1 September 2026. It is the opposite end from a channel with millions of subscribers, and the numbers show what a cold start looks like. All of them are under YouTube's current view-counting rules.

  • A brand-new faceless YouTube channel published 105 Shorts in ten weeks and reached a median of 450 views per Short and 14 subscribers (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, Plotwright, November 2026).
  • On a new channel, viewers watched an average of 21.7 seconds of 38-second Shorts, about 57% of the length (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, Plotwright, November 2026).
  • About one in three people shown a new channel's Short chose to watch it rather than swipe away (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, Plotwright, September to November 2026).
  • In one experiment week, 13 of 14 Shorts missed the view prediction written down before they were posted (Aditya Vora Shorts Report, Plotwright, November 2026).
  • The same approach to retention averaged about 1.5 million views per Short on a channel with an existing audience and a median of 450 on a channel with none. Distribution, not only the edit, decides where a Short starts.

Platform figures from YouTube

  • YouTube Shorts averages over 200 billion daily views, a milestone YouTube CEO Neal Mohan announced at Cannes Lions on 18 June 2025 (YouTube Official Blog). YouTube repeated the figure in its January 2026 letter on the year ahead (YouTube Official Blog, 21 January 2026).
  • Since 31 March 2025, YouTube counts a Shorts view every time a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old measure is still in YouTube Analytics as "engaged views" (YouTube Analytics and Reporting API revision history, 26 March 2025).

That second change matters when you read any Shorts view figure, including mine. Views from before 31 March 2025 and views after it were counted under different rules, so a jump across that date is partly a counting change. On this page the August 2023 and 2024 numbers are under the old rules. August 2025 and everything from 2026 are under the new ones.

How these numbers were measured

  • Every first-party number comes from YouTube Studio or Instagram Insights for the channel and period named next to it. They are self-reported by me, Aditya Vora.
  • The 2026 study of 869 Shorts uses public data only: views, length, date and title read from YouTube on 10 November 2026, with no logins, no API keys and no channel analytics. It covers each channel's most recent Shorts (up to 20) published 13 July to 27 October 2026. 100 channels were tried and 45 had enough Shorts in that window. Channels I work with were left out. The full sample, exclusions and checks are in the method note.
  • Channel totals belong to the channel and the team behind it. On Ankur Warikoo's finance channel, Shaurya Shikhar carried the content and data side and the channel is Ankur's. My part is the craft layer: how a clip gets put together so people stay to the end. More on who did what.
  • Per-Short and per-Reel averages are simple division of the totals above, rounded.
  • Each number was first published on my writing page or LinkedIn on the date given there. This page collects them and is updated when there is a new one.
