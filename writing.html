Notes on short-form

What actually moves numbers on short-form video: retention, formats, and the decisions that get made long before anyone opens a timeline. These started as posts on LinkedIn. They are collected here with the dates they were written, so the numbers stay anchored to when I wrote them.

What the edit is actually for

8 July 2026

Ankur Warikoo said on one of his videos that the channel does a billion views a year, and he called me one of the best short-form editors around. I was glad to hear it. I also want to add a layer to what that work is.

Hand a sixty second clip to a room of editors and you will get clean, mostly correct cuts back. That is a real craft and I have no interest in talking it down.

The part I have spent six years on is the question underneath it. If there are a hundred ways to cut one clip, which one makes somebody connect with it, or stay to the very end? Same footage, same tools. What changes is understanding the content and the person on the other side of the screen, rather than the cut on its own.

That is what sits behind 5B+ views across more than 1,400 Shorts. Better decisions about what a viewer needs to see and when, not faster cuts. The craft is the foundation. Understanding the content is the part I have worked hardest on.

Thirty days with Autofy

9 May 2026

I helped Autofy grow 80% on Instagram in thirty days. Here is how.

Autofy is a car content brand. I came in to fix the content engine: what gets made, what gets cut, and why.

Over the first thirty days, March into April, Instagram views went from 14,020 to 25,206. Shares went from 20 to 209, likes from 176 to 1,001, comments from 15 to 35. On YouTube, April alone did more views, 30,445, than January, February and March put together, which came to 14,397. Watch time went from 51 hours to 188. Impressions went from 16,943 to 167,949. Retention went from 47% to 61%.

Three things changed. I went back through six months of content and pulled the actual signal: which carousels got saves, which reels got shares, which Shorts held retention. Not what felt good, what the numbers said worked. I studied competitors talking to the same audience whose content was genuinely pulling views, which meant the accounts the algorithm was rewarding rather than the ones with the biggest followings. And I started giving feedback on every post and reel before it shipped. After six years of this you can usually tell inside the first three seconds whether something will hold or drop, and anything that could not carry emotion or hold attention got reworked or killed.

Now the part that usually gets left out. Some of those percentages look far more dramatic than they are, because the bases are small. Twenty shares to 209 shares is plus 945%, but a base of twenty is not somewhere you celebrate from. One to two is also 100% growth on paper. Movement is movement, and the goal is bigger numbers with smaller percentages, not the other way around. Month one was really an experiment to work out what this audience accepts. Kartik Asthana, who founded Autofy, and I agreed at the start that getting where we actually want to be would take six months.

Two billion views, and whose they are

3 March 2026

Ankur Warikoo's finance channel passed two billion views on Shorts alone.

Two ways to sit with that number. Every person in India would have to watch more than once. And if each view were a WhatsApp message, your phone would take thirty two years to stop buzzing.

For anyone who wants the data: two billion views across 1,313 Shorts published, which works out to roughly 1.5 million views per Short. Average retention of 85.1%. Two point two million subscribers gained.

I want to be exact about the pronoun there. That is a team number and not mine. Shaurya Shikhar carried the content and data side of it throughout, and the channel is Ankur's. My part is the craft layer: how a clip gets put together so that people stay to the end of it.

If you have watched even one of them, thank you. You are part of the two billion.

My best month two years ago is an average month now

23 September 2025

August 2023 was the best month I had had: 75% average retention across thirty Shorts, and 1.9 million views.

August 2025 was the same shape of month: 86% average retention across thirty Shorts, and 4.4 million views. By then that was just an average month.

Some context for those averages. The weakest Short that month held 74%. The strongest came in at 102%, which is what happens when enough people rewatch. And it took more than 600 days and more than 600 Shorts to turn a best month into an ordinary one.

The takeaway is not complicated. Retention drives performance. When something is easy to keep watching, whether it is teaching you something or just making you laugh, people stay longer and the platform pushes it further.
